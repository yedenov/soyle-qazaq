# Content and language notes

## Editorial scope

The 12 short lessons and 6 articles are original introductory learning examples created with AI assistance. They cover common greetings, self-introduction, selected letters, counting, family, café requests, shopping, asking to repeat, directions, time, weather and a short recap dialogue. The 72 phrase cards are grouped into 6 everyday themes.

They are a demonstrable curriculum with a consistent practice loop, not a comprehensive language syllabus. Lesson completion and application levels are not CEFR certification. The content has not been reviewed by a named professional teacher; qualified editorial review is an explicit next step. No invented teacher, university partnership or learner outcomes are presented.

## Choices

- Use Kazakh Cyrillic for contemporary beginner examples; do not suggest that a Latin transliteration in the brand is an official spelling standard.
- Keep explanations in Russian, with short Kazakh examples and direct Russian glosses.
- Explain polite versus informal forms when they matter.
- Avoid substituting Russian sounds as if they were exact Kazakh pronunciation.
- Label audio as a device capability and do not play a Russian voice under a Kazakh label.
- Treat phrase translations as contextual: for example, `күн` can mean day or sun, and `өтінемін` is please in a request.
- Keep family terms precise: `аға` is older brother, `іні` younger brother, `әпке` older sister.

## Review checklist for a teacher

1. Check every Kazakh phrase and Russian gloss for correctness, register and beginner suitability.
2. Review the sequence and accuracy of grammar explanations.
3. Ensure distractors have exactly one intended answer in their stated context.
4. Expand short sound-recognition examples into teacher-led pronunciation activities.
5. Add licensed native-speaker audio and listening exercises.
6. Pilot with consenting beginners and revise based on observed confusion, without claiming outcomes before evaluation.
